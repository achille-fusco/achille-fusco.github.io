I am a theoretical and computational linguist working in **natural language processing, cognitive modeling, and formal semantics**.

I gained my PhD in **Neurocognitive Science and Philosophy of Mind** at **IUSS Pavia**. I was also Research Fellow at the **University of Florence**, where I contributed to the **Manzini-Savoia corpus** of Italo-Romance dialects.

My research investigates how linguistic structure and meaning can be represented, learned, and evaluated, both in humans and in computational models. I am particularly interested in language models trained under cognitively motivated constraints, in the linguistic properties of tokenization and representation, and in the relationship between formal analyses of grammar and computational modeling.

Recent projects include work on **small language models and linguistically informed neural architectures**, **tokenization strategies and language model evaluation**, and the development of **computational resources and annotation pipelines for Italian and Italo-Romance varieties**. I also work on formal semantic questions concerning attitude predicates, modality, and the syntax–semantics interface.

More broadly, I am interested in research that brings theoretical linguistics and NLP into closer contact: using linguistic theory to better understand computational models, and computational methods to test, scale, and operationalize linguistic hypotheses.
